2. Problem Statement
Background of the Problem
In tropical and high-humidity micro-climates, standard household cooling appliances operate in
total functional isolation. High-power appliances like Inverter Air Conditioners (ACs), BLDC
Ceiling Fans, and Refrigerators run on isolated internal thermostats without exchanging data or
adjusting to dynamic environmental conditions.
During hot and humid monsoon weather ("moist" weather), occupants frequently over-cool
rooms with ACs simply to extract moisture, leading to severe energy inefficiency and high
electricity bills.

Who Faces This Problem?
●​ Urban and Suburban Households: Families in high-humidity tropical regions facing
excessive monthly power bills due to uncoordinated cooling appliances.
●​ Inverter/Battery-Reliant Homes: Residents experiencing abrupt battery inverter tripping
during frequent power outages caused by simultaneous appliance compressor inrush
currents.
●​ Energy Management Researchers & Utilities: Smart grid planners seeking
software-based residential load shedding and peak-demand optimization strategies without
expensive hardware upgrades.

Why Is the Problem Important?
Individual appliances cannot optimize household energy footprint independently. Treating
relative humidity strictly through temperature drops leads to excessive power usage.
Coordinating thermal comfort through wind-chill effects (BLDC Fans), dehumidification modes
(Inverter ACs), and load-deferral (Refrigerator defrost cycles) significantly lowers residential
energy consumption while ensuring uninterrupted operation on solar/inverter backup.
Existing Difficulty
Existing home automation systems operate via rigid, manual, or hard-coded rule sets (if-else
triggers) that fail to capture:
●​ Human comfort constraints expressed in natural, continuous language.
●​ Probabilistic occupancy uncertainty in physical space.
●​ Multi-variable trade-offs between relative humidity, temperature, and power grid
limitations.
Therefore, the proposed project addresses the following core problem:
How can Natural Language Processing, Bayesian reasoning, and Fuzzy Logic control be
integrated into a hybrid software framework to dynamically manage appliance energy
consumption, eliminate over-cooling, and prevent battery inverter tripping during power grid
outages?

3. Proposed Project Title
Smart Home Appliance Energy Management System: A Data-Driven Hybrid AI Approach
for Climate & Load Control
The proposed software-driven platform processes multi-modal time-series micro-climate data
and human comfort commands through a 3-stage artificial intelligence architecture:
1.​ Cognitive NLP Engine: Extracts human intent and thermal comfort boundaries from
natural text prompts.
2.​ Bayesian Belief Network: Infers room occupancy probabilities $P(\text{Occupancy}
\mid E)$ under spatial motion uncertainty to avoid conditioning vacant spaces.
3.​ Fuzzy Logic Control Engine: Evaluates continuous micro-climate variables (Ambient
Temperature, Relative Humidity, Grid Power State) using De Morgan's fuzzy rules to
dynamically adjust AC Compressor Load (%), AC Mode (Cool vs. Dry), BLDC Fan Speed
(Steps 1–5), and Refrigerator Defrost Timing.
4. Research Gap / Existing System Limitation
From the literature survey [1–6], existing energy management solutions primarily focus on
standalone appliance scheduling, direct temperature threshold triggers, or high-cost physical IoT
sensor deployments [1, 2]. They fail to address the combined effect of high humidity and thermal
load on battery inverters during grid outages [1, 5]. Standard systems treat relative humidity as a
secondary variable, causing AC units to run at high compressor loads when operating in Dry
Mode would achieve comfort at a fraction of the power [3, 6]. Furthermore, existing
implementations lack probabilistic reasoning to handle motion sensor uncertainty and do not
offer intuitive natural language interaction for end users [1, 2, 4]. The proposed system fills this
gap by integrating a natural language intent parser [2, 3], Bayesian occupancy inference [1], and
multi-variable Fuzzy Logic control into an integrated, interactive Streamlit simulation
environment [1, 5].
5. Objectives of the Project
1.​ To study multi-modal time-series micro-climate datasets and existing AI control
techniques for home energy optimization.
2.​ To build a Cognitive NLP engine capable of parsing natural human text queries into
operational thermal comfort parameters.
3.​ To develop a Bayesian Belief Network for inferring room occupancy probability under
motion sensor uncertainty.
4.​ To implement a Fuzzy Logic Control Engine for multi-appliance load throttling, mode
selection (Cool vs. Dry), and inverter overload prevention.
5.​ To evaluate system performance using standard evaluation metrics (Accuracy, Precision,

Recall, F1-score, and Confusion Matrix) and present a dashboard using Streamlit.
6. Proposed Methodology
The proposed system follows a step-by-step data processing, probabilistic reasoning, fuzzy
inferencing, and visualization pipeline.

​
Brief Description of Each Step
●​ Data Collection: A 30-day (720-hour) micro-climate time-series dataset
(smart_home_climate_data.csv) simulating tropical temperature swings ,sensor triggers,
and power grid status (Mains vs. Inverter) is generated.
●​ Data Preprocessing: Missing values are handled, continuous climate variables are
normalized, and textual human commands are lowercased, tokenized, and mapped to
domain features.
●​ Feature Selection: Key indicators impacting appliance power state—such as
ambient_temp, relative_humidity, motion_sensor, and grid_power—are isolated for
inferencing.
●​ Model Training & Fitting: ML models (Random Forest, Decision Trees, Naive Bayes,
KNN) and Fuzzy Rule bases are trained and fitted on the multi-modal dataset.
●​ Testing & Evaluation: System outputs are evaluated using multi-class metrics including
Accuracy, Precision, Recall, F1-Score, and Confusion Matrix visual maps.
●​ Digital Footprint & Energy Dashboard: Results, real-time slider simulations, grid
failover switches, and performance plots are served through an interactive Streamlit web
application.

8. Expected Outcome
The proposed system will successfully optimize household energy consumption across Inverter
ACs, BLDC Fans, and Refrigerators under varying micro-climate conditions and grid states. The
system expects to achieve:
1.​ Zero Inverter Overload Trips: 100% suppression of simultaneous heavy compressor
starts during grid outages (grid_power = 0).
2.​ Dehumidification Efficiency: Automatic switching to AC Dry Mode during
high-humidity monsoons, reducing over-cooling and saving power.
3.​ High ML Classification Performance: High prediction accuracy (95%) across
multi-class operating modes verified using Confusion Matrices, Precision, Recall, and
F1-Scores.
4.​ Interactive GUI: A fully functional Streamlit dashboard allowing users to input natural
language commands, adjust real-time climate sliders, toggle power grid states, and
visualize AI outputs.
